# DMFlow website design reference — public-safe swipe guide

Use these products as **layout and interaction references**, not as assets to copy. Reuse patterns: information hierarchy, section rhythm, card density, product-demo framing and interaction ideas. Do not copy logos, proprietary screenshots, illustrations, testimonials or exact copy.

## Best references

### Motion — https://motionapp.com
**Steal the pattern:** brutally clear hero, one outcome-focused headline, product UI immediately below it, proof close to the CTA.

**DMFlow use:** hero should say the job in plain language, then show Scout's shortlist + evidence + outreach approval UI before explaining all three products.

### Insense — https://insense.pro
**Steal the pattern:** creator-content energy in the hero, recognizable UGC framing, strong agency/brand orientation, product credibility badges.

**DMFlow use:** add a creator-card/video-strip feel around Scout so it reads as creator software, not generic B2B software.

### Cohley — https://cohley.com
**Steal the pattern:** concise “without chaos” positioning, workflow broken into brief → match → manage → activate → analyze, proof embedded beside capabilities.

**DMFlow use:** organize Scout around a visible recruitment workflow rather than a feature list.

### minisocial — https://minisocial.com
**Steal the pattern:** human/creator personality, slightly playful typography, lots of real-world creator language, big operational proof.

**DMFlow use:** loosen the current “developer tool” vibe. Keep the dark product UI, but make marketing sections warmer and more human.

### Archive — https://archive.com
**Steal the pattern:** “manual vs with product” contrast, creator-program language, simple operational benefits and AI framed around specific jobs.

**DMFlow use:** keep the existing before/after section, but anchor it to research, vetting and recruitment rather than generic productivity.

### SARAL — https://getsaral.com
**Steal the pattern:** an opinionated operating-system story, dashboard screenshots, strong workflow education.

**DMFlow use:** Campaigns should eventually feel like an operator console, not another Manychat clone.

### Foreplay — https://foreplay.co
**Steal the pattern:** visual content grids, swipe-file/product screenshots, workflow storytelling, “mess before / organized after.”

**DMFlow use:** Scout candidate results should visually resemble a creative research board — thumbnail/card first, evidence second.

### MagicBrief — https://magicbrief.com
**Steal the pattern:** creative-tech polish, dense visual browsing UI, purple/bright accent used only where it matters, product screenshot dominates.

**DMFlow use:** inspiration for the Scout results workspace and “campaign brief → creators” experience.

### Superads — https://superads.ai
**Steal the pattern:** high-contrast editorial headline, one accent color, extremely clear problem statements, product capability blocks.

**DMFlow use:** strongest reference for typography and section hierarchy.

### JoinBrands — https://joinbrands.com
**Steal the pattern:** creator-category browsing, concrete creator cards, audience segmentation.

**DMFlow use:** useful for how creator cards should scan, but DMFlow should remain agency-operator focused instead of marketplace-first.

### Influee — https://influee.co
**Steal the pattern:** tangible offer framing, simple “launch → creators → content” sequence, visual creator proof.

**DMFlow use:** copy the *clarity* of the process, not the marketplace promise.

## Recommended DMFlow visual recipe

**70% Motion/Superads** — clean hierarchy, product-first SaaS polish.

**20% Insense/minisocial** — creator personality, human faces/video framing, warm editorial moments.

**10% Foreplay/MagicBrief** — dense creative-research cards inside the actual app.

Keep one signature color. The current acid-lime is differentiated enough. Use cream/off-white marketing surfaces against the dark app mockups so the creator visuals do not disappear into one black page.

## Homepage order

1. Hero: “Find creators worth contacting — and know why.”
2. Interactive-looking Scout shortlist mockup.
3. One sentence: built for smaller UGC agencies doing the research manually.
4. Before/after workflow.
5. 3-step demo: brief → evidence-backed shortlist → reviewed outreach.
6. Proof/validation section with **real pilot metrics only**; until then, label it “what we measure.”
7. Three products with Scout visually dominant.
8. API/safety trust strip: no scraping, no unsolicited Instagram automation, human approval.
9. Pilot CTA.

## What not to copy

- competitor logos as your own proof;
- screenshots from their dashboards;
- creator photos without rights;
- exact headlines/testimonials;
- fake customer logos or fake metrics;
- generic gradient blobs everywhere.

The goal is recognizable category design with a distinct DMFlow operating model.
